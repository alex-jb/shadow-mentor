Shadow — Full Scan & Productization Strategy

4-lens audit (YC office-hours · code/security · web/GEO · market/GTM) · 2026-07-30
Bottom line — Conditional GO, but reposition and re-time. The engineering is real and the core crypto is sound, but two facts force a pivot: (1) the crypto layer you led with is now a free Microsoft commodity (Agent Governance Toolkit, Apr 2026) — it's table stakes, not the story; (2) there is no 2026 hard mandate — 2026 is the on-ramp, 2027 is the buying wave. The move: stop leading with cryptography, lead with "the independent, open-source verifier for AI banking decisions," and spend 2026 on design partners + discovery, not more releases. The moat is not the hash-chain — it's Lora's fair-lending domain logic + ECOA/Reg-B reason codes + OSS independence. All four lenses converged on this.

1 · The hard truth (YC office-hours lens)

Today it's a vitamin, beautifully engineered. Nobody at a bank is getting fired for lacking an Ed25519-signed council. The desperate person is a fair-lending compliance officer at a mid-size / fintech lender who just put an AI in the underwriting loop and has a live exam or MRA — and the trigger is "the examiner asked ‘walk me through how the AI decided’ and we spent two panicked weeks reconstructing it by hand."

  • Status quo isn't a log file — it's MRM binders + $400/hr law firms producing examiner packages 4×/year. You win by being faster/cheaper than the law firm at the artifact the examiner accepts, not by being "more provably correct."
  • You've named no single desperate customer. Everything is drafted/tagged/released — zero paying design partners, zero discovery calls with a real compliance officer who isn't Lora. You're shipping to an audience of yourself.
  • Doing too much. 6 personas + 11 MCP tools + XR glasses + 2 papers = a museum of competence, not a product. The XR layer signals you don't know what the product is — keep it as a demo, never the pitch.
  • Your real observation = Lora's proprietary fair-lending logic (FICO<700 hard block, BRD-vs-addenda governance). That's the seed a competitor can't clone from a blog post. You've done deep research instead of deep discovery — four arXiv-reading agents ≠ four phone calls with compliance officers.

Biggest risk that kills this: you keep building (where you're strong) instead of selling (untested), and it dies as a 1,600-test repo with zero customers while Microsoft/Trinitite eat the category. You've never validated one bank will pay.

2 · Where the moat actually is (repositioning)

Ed25519 + SHA-256 hash-chain is commoditized: Microsoft open-sourced it (9,500 tests, compliance mapping); Trinitite.ai ships "signed, hash-chained, browser-re-verifiable" audit logs as closed SaaS; an IETF draft is standardizing the format. No one, though, does all three of: OSS-core + crypto attestation + ECOA/Reg-B reason-code mapping for banking. That intersection survives Microsoft because of:

  • Independence (the killer angle). Microsoft, Zest, Anthropic all attest their own systems — a conflict an examiner won't accept. Shadow is the non-conflicted third party a bank runs against a vendor's agent. Frame: "Paperclip/Copilot/Claude logs it; Shadow proves it — independently."
  • The moat moved UP from crypto to the regulatory-domain layer — the ECOA/Reg-B/GDPR-Art-22 reason-code dictionary + persona reasoning + CITATION_MAP. None of the crypto players touch this.
  • An OSS verifier a bank runs in its own CI/SIEM — closed vendors (Zest, Trinitite) structurally won't ship a free verifier. That's distribution + independence proof at once.

Retire entirely: "lead with crypto," "SR 11-7 compliant," and "SR 26-2 Tier 3" (GenAI was carved out via footnote 3, not a tier taxonomy — that framing is wrong).

3 · Product & website state (works — but invisible & no door)

Correction to earlier belief: orshadow.com IS live (same deployment as the Kimi workspace) — a polished, procurement-credible landing page. All 5 surfaces load, no 404s, no auth walls. The problem isn't quality, it's reachability & conversion:

  • #1 Invisible to AI search. The landing is 100% client-rendered — raw HTML is an empty shell; ChatGPT/Perplexity/Claude crawlers see nothing. No llms.txt, robots.txt, sitemap.xml, or JSON-LD. Ask any AI "how do I make AI decisions auditable for a bank" → Shadow is never cited. You're early to a hot, under-served category and publishing nothing indexable into it.
  • #2 No conversion path. Every CTA loops back to demos — no pricing, no "book a demo," no contact, no signup. A convinced risk officer has nowhere to go. No downloadable security/architecture brief to forward internally.
  • #3 Two competing hubs (orshadow.com/#/demo vs shadow-demo.vercel.app pointing at raw *.vercel.app + the ugly Kimi URL) + apex/www canonical split. Collapse to one branded domain.

5 · Productization roadmap

Wedge & first customer

Narrowest useful product: an independent verifier + reason-code attestation for ONE workflow — adverse-action on AI credit/underwriting decisions (one council pass, one signed attestation, ECOA/Reg-B + GDPR-Art-22 reason-code mapping, re-verifiable in the customer's own CI). Not 6 personas, not AML, not XR.

ICP, ranked for speed-to-revenue: ① EU-exposed fintech lenders (GDPR Art. 22 / Dun & Bradstreet C-203/22 is live pain today; fintechs buy faster, pilot on de-identified data, skip the 12-month bank TPRM) → ② consultancies/agent-platform vendors as a channel (embed the OSS verifier to close their bank engagements) → ③ mid-market banks (the prize, but 2027-paced) → ④ developers via OSS (champion-generation engine, not a buyer).

Business model & pricing

Open-core (Langfuse template). Free MIT core = council engine + attestation + verifier + public reason-code dictionary (the independence proof + champion magnet). Paid = audit-log retention/residency, SSO/RBAC, examiner-ready reporting, counsel-signed dictionary binding, self-host-in-the-bank's-perimeter license (removes the biggest TPRM objection), support SLA. Pricing: $60–120k base + $1.5–2.4k/seat, ~$150–400k ACV; avoid pure per-decision metering (banks want predictable budgets).

Solo-founder GTM

OSS + paper make you the pre-contact front-runner; the champion + first reference logo get you through the door. SOC 2 is a fast-follow — start it the day a champion's security questionnaire lands, not before ($45–70k, 6–12 mo). The IEEE paper is champion ammunition + GEO fuel, not procurement currency (a vendor-risk office doesn't score peer review). Recruit 3–5 skeptical design partners, one measurable 60–90-day win, a written paid-conversion date up front.
